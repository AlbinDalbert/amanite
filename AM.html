Animal Machines

Keaton snapped the AM Transmitter to his hip—balanced, metallic, still cold from his backpack. The highland plateau stretched out in front of him. Flat and rocky, decorated with pines, ferns and moss. Yet, the herd was still here. Why? They should have migrated many weeks ago. Something had stalled them. 

He crouched behind a dead pine tree, dropped his bag, and pulled down the sunshade over his visor. The noon sun was harsh in this region. The animal-machines sprawled the landscape, like dozens and dozens of rocks and boulders, half-covered with moss and ferns. Silent. Still. Except for a low, constant hum. Only audible when the wind paused. A reminder of their machinery.

“Took you long enough.” Sam buzzed in over the radio.

“Don’t spill your ashes.” Keaton replied as he started taking out the AM Scanner parts from the bag.

“Just saying, I’m bored. Nothing interesting is happening here, and I’m getting cooked alive in this suite and I got rocks and roots poking me everywhere. Just like I told you on the ship… Have you found the broken Bizon yet?”

“Don’t rush me. And stop complaining, we don’t want ‘interesting’, we want success. Would you rather have stayed back in the city than perched up on a canyon in the wilds looking at Bizons?”

“Touché.”

“I don’t want a herd freakout” Keaton continued as he started assembling. Connecting the power supply with the base, snapping on the large antenna. And lastly, he picked up three small drones from the bag. They mimicked hummingbirds, so small that he could fit all in his hand. The wing noise from the drones shouldn’t trigger the Bizons.

“Relax Keet, we’ve never had a freakout.”

“That’s because I’m careful.” Keaton placed the drones on the base. “How many have you spotted?”

There was a lingering silence before Sam replied. “Five hundred.” 

Keaton could hear the suppressed grin on Sam's voice and he sighed loud enough for the radio to pick it up. “Seriously, how many?”

“Ok, forty-six.” 

Keaton considered the number for a moment. That was a lot. “Is that true?”

“Swear to the metal. It’s actually forty-six.”

Keaton glanced across the open landscape and up the vertical rock face on the other side. There he could see the small dot on the edge where Sam was peaking over. “What’s the range?”

“The whole area might be half a league along the rock face.  Mainly to your left. Push them out at 224 degrees.” vivian

Keaton crouched around the tree and pushed the scanner out a few meters in front of the tree, he angled it at 224. Half a league. This could take a while. He went back to his bag and picked up two more drones. Placed them next to the others on the scanner base. A colourful array of small headless flapping cylinders. He returned to the dead tree and activated the scanner. The base buzzed  and the drones hummed to life.
